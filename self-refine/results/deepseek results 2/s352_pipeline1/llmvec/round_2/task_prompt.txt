        Tester feedback:
        The cleanup loop assumes remaining elements are always a multiple of 5, but `LEN_1D % 20` can leave 5, 10, or 15 elements — which are multiples of 5, so that's fine. However, the real issue is that `LEN_1D` itself may not be a multiple of 5, causing out-of-bounds access in the cleanup loop (`i + 1` through `i + 4` may exceed `LEN_1D`).

More critically, the return value mismatch suggests the accumulation logic differs from the reference. The original `s352` does a simple dot product `dot += a[i]*b[i]` for all `i` in `[0, LEN_1D)`, not grouped by 5.

- **Root cause**: The original loop is a plain dot product over all `LEN_1D` elements. The candidate incorrectly structures cleanup in steps of 5, which can read out-of-bounds and produce wrong results when `LEN_1D` is not a multiple of 5 (or 20).
- **Fix**: Replace the cleanup loop with a simple scalar loop `for (; i < LEN_1D; i++) dot += a[i] * b[i];` — no step of 5, no `i+1..i+4` accesses.
